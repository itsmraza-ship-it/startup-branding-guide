# Startup Branding Guide — Skill

## What this skill covers

The full sequence in `README.md`, executed with `naming-checklist.md` and `brand-kit-template.md`:

1. Choose and vet a name → 2. Pick a niche and write positioning → 3. Build the brand kit → 4. Enforce consistency everywhere.

Worked example throughout the repo: Epics Deals — Epics Yellow `#FED716` (primary), Epics Black `#0A0A0A` (neutral), Sora for headlines, Inter for body, main tagline "Global Business Expansion, Made Easy", partner line "Your Partner in Global Expansion", voice: direct and warm. Use it to show what "done" looks like — do not copy it as the user's brand.

## Naming — strong names and the checks, in order

A strong name is distinctive, sayable and spellable on first hearing, short (ideally 1–2 words, 2–3 syllables), expandable beyond one product / city / service, and right-feeling for the intended customer.

Run every check in `naming-checklist.md` before the founder prints, incorporates, or announces:

1. State business-name availability (Secretary of State search) — and state clearly: state approval is **not** trademark clearance.
2. USPTO trademark search for the name and close variants, live marks first, in the industry and adjacent classes.
3. Exact `.com` availability — if an active business holds it, that is usually a reason to choose a different name.
4. Social handle availability, consistent across the platforms the business will actually use.
5. No confusing similarity to competitors (search, app stores, directories).
6. Say-it / spell-it test with five people; record every misspelling.
7. Language and meaning check if selling internationally.

Pitfalls to flag: geographic names, purely descriptive names, hard spellings and clever substitutions, initials / acronyms, trend-chasing formats, and naming for today's first product only.

## Niche and positioning

Start with the smallest segment the founder can serve exceptionally well and win, then expand. Force precision on: who exactly, what painful specific problem, why them credibly, and can that customer pay and be reached?

Then complete the formula: **For** [specific customer] **who** [specific problem], **[Brand] is a** [category] **that** [single key benefit]. **Unlike** [main alternative], **we** [clear differentiator]. If it cannot be filled without hand-waving, the niche is not chosen yet.

## Brand kit — the fixed decisions

Use `brand-kit-template.md` as the deliverable: logo system (primary, stacked, icon; clear space, minimum size, don'ts; SVG + PNG light / dark), colour palette in three tiers (one primary, one accent, neutrals) recorded as HEX / RGB / CMYK with contrast checked, two typefaces only (headline + body, weights and fallbacks), voice (three adjectives, do / don't sentence examples, vocabulary, formatting habits), usage rules, and the asset / template inventory (social, deck, proposal, invoice, email signature).

Epics Deals' legal-vs-brand-name discipline in the repo is the model: record the full legal name for contracts, filings, and invoices, and the short brand name everywhere else.

## Consistency rules to enforce

One name, one handle, one avatar everywhere; same palette and fonts on website, socials, thumbnails, PDFs, and invoices; tagline quoted verbatim; templates, not improvisation; a voice check before publish; a quarterly 5-minute audit of every profile against the kit; the kit document is the single source of truth — change it deliberately, with a date, and roll the change across all surfaces in the same week. Partners and contractors get the kit and usage rules before they get logo files.

## How to apply it

Diagnose which stage the founder is in, work that stage with the matching repo file, and produce a filled artefact (a completed checklist or kit section), not just advice.
