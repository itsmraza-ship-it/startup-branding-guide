# Naming Checklist

Vet a company name end-to-end before you incorporate, print, or announce anything. Work top to bottom — the cheap checks come first, and a name that fails an early step should be dropped, not rescued.

## Step 1 — The Basics (5 minutes, kills most bad names)

- [ ] **Say it out loud.** It is easy and natural to pronounce on the first try
- [ ] **Spell-it test.** Said once to 5 people, they can write it down correctly (note every misspelling: [MISSPELLINGS HEARD])
- [ ] **Hear-it test.** Heard once (call / podcast / referral), a stranger can find it via search by typing what they heard
- [ ] **Short enough.** Ideally 1–2 words, 2–3 syllables — fits a logo, a handle, and a conversation
- [ ] **No unwanted readings.** No awkward abbreviations, initials, or second meanings when written without spaces (`yourname` as one string)
- [ ] **Not a trend clone.** Does not depend on a fashionable suffix/format ("-ify", "get-", dropped vowels) that dates it or merges it into a crowd
- [ ] **Room to grow.** Does not lock the company to one product, one city, one audience, or a founder's name you may outgrow

## Step 2 — Competitor and Confusion Check

- [ ] **Google the exact name.** First page reviewed — who already owns this name in search?
- [ ] **Google `name + your industry`** (e.g., `name + LLC formation`). No established company in your space with the same or a near-identical name
- [ ] **Google close variants** — plural/singular, with and without spaces, common misspellings, the same name with "co", "inc", "labs", "group" added
- [ ] **App stores / marketplaces checked** (if relevant to your industry): [Apple App Store / Google Play / Amazon / OTHER]
- [ ] **Industry directories checked:** [DIRECTORIES CHECKED]
- [ ] **One-letter / one-sound test.** No competitor whose name differs by a single letter or sound — customers, reviews, and payments *will* get confused
- [ ] **Notes:** [WHAT YOU FOUND]

## Step 3 — State Business-Name Availability

- [ ] **Formation state entity search completed:** [STATE] Secretary of State business search, on [DATE]
- [ ] Exact legal name **[NAME, e.g., "________ LLC"]** shows as available
- [ ] No existing entity with a confusingly similar name in the state results
- [ ] Name meets state rules (contains "LLC" / "Limited Liability Company" or permitted designator if an LLC; no restricted words like "bank", "insurance", "university" without approval)
- [ ] **Other states checked** (if you will register/operate there soon): [STATES]
- [ ] ⚠️ **Understood:** state availability is *not* trademark clearance — passing this step alone does not give you the right to use the name commercially

## Step 4 — Trademark Search (USPTO)

- [ ] **USPTO Trademark Search (TESS) completed** on [DATE] by [NAME]
- [ ] Exact name searched — no live mark in your or an adjacent class of goods/services
- [ ] Close variants searched (spelling, phonetic equivalents, plural/singular, translated equivalents if relevant): [VARIANTS SEARCHED]
- [ ] Relevant international classes identified: [CLASS NUMBERS, e.g., 35 — business services; 36 — financial; 42 — software]
- [ ] Dead/abandoned marks reviewed — noted whether any could be revived or still used in commerce: [NOTES]
- [ ] **Common-law check.** Unregistered users found in Step 2 reviewed — a business using the name without a federal registration can still have rights where it operates
- [ ] **International check** (if you sell abroad): [EUIPO / UK IPO / WIPO Global Brand Database — DATABASES CHECKED]
- [ ] Any hit that is even arguably close has been reviewed by a trademark attorney — **decision:** [CLEAR / RISK ACCEPTED IN WRITING / NAME DROPPED]
- [ ] Filing planned: [ ] Intent-to-use application · [ ] Use-based application · [ ] After launch — target date: [DATE]

## Step 5 — Domain

- [ ] **Exact `.com` checked:** [yourname.com] — status: [ ] Available to register · [ ] Parked/for sale at $[PRICE] · [ ] In active use by [WHO]
- [ ] If the `.com` is in active use by another business → default decision is **pick a different name** (exceptions must be a conscious, written decision: [REASON])
- [ ] Domain registered (not just checked): registrar [REGISTRAR], account owner [NAME/EMAIL], auto-renew **on**
- [ ] Useful variants registered to prevent confusion/typosquatting: [ ] plural/singular · [ ] common misspelling · [ ] [OTHER, e.g., .co / country domain]
- [ ] Domain privacy protection enabled
- [ ] ⚠️ Checked the domain's **history** (Wayback Machine) — not previously used for spam, a failed scandal brand, or an unrelated adult/gambling site: [ ] Clean · [ ] Issue: [NOTE]

## Step 7 — Language, Culture, and Sound

- [ ] Name (and its common misspellings) checked for unintended meanings in your customers' main languages: [LANGUAGES CHECKED]
- [ ] Pronunciation is workable for non-native speakers of your main market (if international)
- [ ] No negative association with a news event, meme, slang term, or public figure: [ ] Checked via search/news on [DATE]
- [ ] Sounds right for the brand position (premium / playful / technical / trustworthy — circle yours) when spoken in your positioning sentence

## Step 8 — Final Gut Checks

- [ ] **Phone test:** "Hi, I'm calling from [NAME]" — sounds like a company a stranger would trust with money
- [ ] **Email test:** `hello@[yourname].com` / `support@[yourname].com` look professional and can't be misread
- [ ] **Logo test:** the name set in plain text (no design) already looks balanced — not too long for a header, app icon label, or invoice
- [ ] **Five-year test:** the name still works if you double your product line, enter a second country, or get acquired — [ ] Yes
- [ ] **Founder-bias check:** at least [3–5] people *outside* the founding team (ideally target customers) heard the name cold and reacted: [SUMMARY OF REACTIONS]

## Step 9 — Lock It In

- [ ] Name approved by: [NAMES / DATE]
- [ ] Legal entity filed (or filing instructed) under the exact cleared name
- [ ] Trademark application filed or calendared (Step 4)
- [ ] Domain(s) registered and in the company account (Step 5)
- [ ] All handles claimed and in the password manager (Step 6)
- [ ] Name, legal name, domain, and handles recorded in the brand kit ([brand-kit-template.md](brand-kit-template.md), Section 1 and Section 7)
- [ ] Announcement, website, and printing held until every box above was checked: [ ] Confirmed

---

**Fail-fast rule:** a fail at Step 3, 4, 5 (`.com` in active use), or 6 (confusing active handle on a key platform) means the name is dropped and you return to the candidate list. Fixing a name is free *before* launch and expensive forever after it.
